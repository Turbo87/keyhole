# GDPR obligations for pilot data and IGC files

Research for [#14](https://github.com/Turbo87/keyhole/issues/14). The question is what the GDPR requires of Keyhole for published pilot data, stored IGC files, and admin accounts, and whether Keyhole may use processors hosted in the US. Keyhole is operated by one person in Germany. It publishes results with pilot names, clubs, and gliders for every competition, stores IGC files that contain the pilot name, glider registration, and logger serial number, and holds email-only accounts for competition admins and scorers. Pilots have no accounts. Facts were checked against the GDPR, European Commission decisions, EDPB documents, German supervisory authority guidance, German statutes, and CJEU case law on 2026-10-30. Statements marked **Interpretation** are the author's reading of those sources and not verified fact. None of this is legal advice.

## Summary

US-hosted processors are usable today. The EU-US Data Privacy Framework (DPF) adequacy decision is in force, so transfers to a DPF-certified US company need no transfer impact assessment, only an Article 28 processor agreement and a mention in the privacy policy. The decision is under real pressure from a pending appeal at the Court of Justice and from the US Supreme Court's removal of FTC independence, so any US processor should also have Standard Contractual Clauses in its DPA as a fallback, and switching away should stay cheap. Publishing results with names and clubs can rest on legitimate interest under Article 6(1)(f), which German authorities accept for competition results. The same authorities limit that to name and club, require that participants are informed, and say online publication must end after an appropriate time. That makes the permanent public archive and public IGC downloads the legally weakest parts of the design. Keyhole determines essential means for every competition (all results are public, which data is shown, how long it is kept), so it is most plausibly a controller for publication rather than a pure processor, either alone or jointly with each organiser. Keyhole needs a privacy policy covering Articles 13 and 14, an Impressum under § 5 DDG and § 18 MStV, a record of processing activities, and processor agreements with its own hosting and email providers. No statute requires keeping IGC files or results, and storage limitation requires a defined retention period for each. Role allocation, the archive, and the terms with organisers are the points where a lawyer is worth paying for.

## Transfers to US processors

### DPF status as of 2026-10-30

The Commission adopted the adequacy decision for the DPF on 10 July 2023 as [Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1795](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32023D1795). It covers only US organisations that have self-certified and appear on the [DPF list](https://www.dataprivacyframework.gov/list). Certification must be renewed every year and must cover the data in question ([EDPB DPF FAQ for businesses v2.0, 15 January 2026](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/other-guidance/eu-us-data-privacy-framework-faq-for-european-businesses-version-20_en), [DSK application notes, 4 September 2023](https://datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/ah/230904_DSK_Ah_EU_US.pdf)). Transfers based on the adequacy decision need no supplementary measures ([EDPB information note, July 2023](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2023-07/edpb_informationnoteadequacydecisionus_en.pdf)). An Article 28 agreement is still required for a certified processor, and the privacy policy must name the recipients and state that the transfer relies on the DPF ([EDPB DPF FAQ v2.0](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-01/edpb_dpf_faq-for-businesses_v2_en.pdf)).

The Commission's [first periodic review](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/25695177-8073-4ce3-bf81-eb816dc6b468_en?filename=Report+on+the+first+periodic+review+of+the+functioning+of+the+adequacy+decision+on+the+EU-US+Data+Privacy+Framework.pdf) (COM(2024) 451, 9 October 2024) found the framework functioning and set the next review three years later. Three risks are open:

- The General Court dismissed Philippe Latombe's annulment action in T-553/23 on 3 September 2025. Latombe appealed on 31 October 2025 as case C-703/25 P and asks the Court of Justice to annul the decision ([OJ notice C/2025/6610](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2025/6610/oj/eng)). EUR-Lex blocked automated fetching, so the notice was confirmed only through search indexes. No judgment was found as of the check date.
- On 29 June 2026 the US Supreme Court held in Trump v. Slaughter that FTC commissioners are removable at will. The EDPB wrote to the Commission on 31 July 2026 that an independent supervisory authority is a key element under Article 45(2)(b), that recitals 58 to 60 of the decision rely on the FTC's for-cause removal protection, and asked the Commission to "closely assess whether this development affects the functioning" of the decision ([EDPB letter](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-08/edpb_letter_20260731_us_supremecourt_judgment_trump_v_slaughter_en.pdf)). No Commission response was found.
- The DSK said at adoption that it could not predict whether the DPF would share the fate of Safe Harbor and Privacy Shield, and that the decision is valid EU law until repealed or annulled ([DSK press release](https://datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/pm/230904_DSK_PM_Anwendungshinweise_EU_US.pdf)).

**Interpretation:** Relying on the DPF is lawful today, but the probability of a third invalidation is not small. If the Court annuls the decision there may be no grace period, as with Schrems II ([C-311/18](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-311/18)). A US processor is acceptable only if leaving it is a configuration change or a short migration.

### Standard Contractual Clauses and transfer impact assessments

For US recipients that are not DPF-certified, or as a fallback, Article 46 allows the Commission's [Standard Contractual Clauses (Decision 2021/914)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2021/914/oj). Module 2 (controller to processor) or module 3 (processor to processor) would apply to Keyhole. After Schrems II the exporter must assess whether third-country law undermines the clauses and add supplementary measures if it does, following the steps in [EDPB Recommendations 01/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2021-06/edpb_recommendations_202001vo.2.0_supplementarymeasurestransferstools_en.pdf). The DSK notes that for non-certified US recipients the Commission's findings on US safeguards in the adequacy decision can be taken into account in that assessment ([DSK application notes](https://datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/ah/230904_DSK_Ah_EU_US.pdf)). The Commission's [SCC Q&A](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/new-standard-contractual-clauses-questions-and-answers-overview_en) states that the 2021 clauses do not fit importers already subject to the GDPR under Article 3(2), and the announced replacement set had not been found adopted as of the check date.

**Interpretation:** For a tiny operator the practical approach is to pick providers that are DPF-certified and whose DPA already incorporates SCCs that apply if the DPF falls. Large US providers usually publish their own transfer assessment documentation. A short written TIA that relies on that documentation and on the Commission's findings, kept with the record of processing, is proportionate to Keyhole's data (names, clubs, flight tracks, admin emails). Pilot tracks are not special category data under Article 9.

### US companies with EU regions

A transfer under Chapter V requires that data is disclosed or made available to an importer in a third country ([EDPB Guidelines 05/2021, para. 9](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-02/edpb_guidelines_05-2021_interplay_between_the_application_of_art3-chapter_v_of_the_gdpr_v2_en_0.pdf)). An EU subsidiary of a US company storing data in an EU region is not itself a transfer, but remote access from the US is, and the risk of third-country government access still has to be considered under Article 32 and accountability. **Interpretation:** an EU region of a US provider reduces but does not remove the transfer analysis, so it is no substitute for the DPA and SCC check.

### Keyhole's own processors

Every service that handles personal data for Keyhole (hosting, database, object storage for IGC files, backups, email, error tracking) is a processor and needs an agreement that covers the items in [Article 28(3)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj). The agreement may be in electronic form ([Article 28(9)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)), so accepting a provider's standard DPA online is sufficient. Scaleway publishes a [DPA](https://www-uploads.scaleway.com/DPA_2024_ENG_b0abb5cc26.pdf) that is part of its contract, and the email-provider research already found that Scaleway TEM keeps data in France. Using Scaleway therefore involves no third-country transfer.

## Legal basis for publishing pilot data

Keyhole has no contract with pilots, so [Article 6(1)(b)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) is not available to Keyhole itself. **Interpretation:** an organiser may be able to rely on 6(1)(b) through its entry contract with the pilot, but only for processing that is necessary for running the competition, and publication on a permanent public archive goes beyond that. Consent under 6(1)(a) fits badly because a withdrawn consent would force removal from published rankings and organisers rarely collect valid consent.

The remaining basis is legitimate interest under 6(1)(f). The CJEU held in [C-621/22 KNLTB](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62022CJ0621) (4 October 2024) that any lawful interest can qualify, but the processing must be strictly necessary for it and must not be overridden by the data subject's interests, with weight on what the data subject could reasonably expect. The case concerned a sports federation passing member data to sponsors. The [EDPB Guidelines 1/2024](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202401_legitimateinterest_en.pdf) on 6(1)(f) apply the same three-step test and remain a consultation version.

German supervisory guidance on sports results is specific. The [LfDI Baden-Württemberg guidance "Datenschutz im Verein"](https://www.baden-wuerttemberg.datenschutz.de/orientierungshilfe-datenschutz-verein/) (Stand 24 June 2020) says:

- Publishing match results is generally necessary for an association's purpose and can rest on 6(1)(f).
- Results of external competition participants may exceptionally be published on the internet without consent, "kurzzeitig", if participants are informed and no overriding interest applies in the individual case.
- The permitted duration depends on the significance of the event and the public's interest, and data must be deleted after an appropriate time.
- Only last name, first name, and club membership are covered, and year of birth only in justified cases.

**Interpretation:** Live and recent results with name, club, glider type, and competition number are well supported. Long-term publication is defensible for national championships and records with lasting public interest, and weaker for small club competitions. Public IGC downloads expose detailed location tracks that the BW guidance does not cover, and need their own balancing. The common practice of platforms like SoaringSpot and WeGlide helps establish reasonable expectations among pilots but is not a legal basis on its own. Whether a permanent archive of every competition with downloadable tracks passes the balancing test is the main question for a lawyer.

## Controller and processor roles

The controller is whoever determines purposes and essential means. The EDPB lists the type of data, the duration of processing, the recipients, and the categories of data subjects as essential means, and says a processor may offer a predefined service only if the controller actively approves it and the processor cannot later change the essential elements ([EDPB Guidelines 07/2020, paras. 30 and 40](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf)). A provider of a platform that others configure for their own purposes is a joint controller if it takes part in determining purposes and means, and a processor otherwise (paras. 65 to 67 and footnote 29, citing [Wirtschaftsakademie C-210/16](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-210/16) and [Fashion ID C-40/17](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-40/17)). A contract that labels a party a processor does not change factual control (para. 29).

**Interpretation:** Keyhole decides that every competition is public, what the public pages show, whether IGC files are downloadable, and how long archives stay online. Those are essential means. The organiser decides to run the competition, which pilots take part, and to use Keyhole. That pattern points to joint controllership under [Article 26](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) for the publication, or to Keyhole as an independent controller for the public archive. A pure Article 28 processor model would require organisers to control visibility and retention, which contradicts "all competitions are public". The practical consequences:

- A per-competition negotiated DPA is not needed. A standard arrangement accepted when an organiser creates a competition is sufficient, either as Article 26 terms or as Article 28 terms in the ToS, depending on the chosen model.
- Under Article 26 the arrangement must allocate who informs pilots and who handles requests, and its essence must be available to pilots. Pilots can still exercise their rights against either party (Article 26(3)).
- If Keyhole ends up as a processor for some data (for example private draft data before publication), Article 28(10) makes it a controller for any processing beyond the organiser's instructions.

For admin and scorer accounts Keyhole is the sole controller, with 6(1)(b) as the basis for providing the service to the account holder.

## Privacy policy and Impressum

The privacy policy must cover the [Article 13](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) items for data collected from the person (admin accounts, server logs, any direct pilot upload) and the Article 14 items for data received from organisers (pilot names, clubs, IGC files). That means controller identity and contact details, purposes and legal bases, the legitimate interests pursued, recipients including every processor and their transfer basis (DPF or SCCs), retention periods or the criteria for them, the data subject rights including the right to object under Article 21, the right to complain to a supervisory authority, and for Article 14 the categories of data and their source. The right to object must be presented clearly and separately (Article 21(4)). Keyhole has no contact details for pilots, so individual notification under Article 14 is likely impossible, and Article 14(5)(b) then requires the information to be made publicly available ([Article 29 WP transparency guidelines WP260 rev.01](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/items/622227)). **Interpretation:** a public privacy policy plus a short notice that organisers include in their entry documents covers this. A session cookie for login is strictly necessary and needs no consent under § 25(2) TDDDG.

The Impressum follows [§ 5 DDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ddg/__5.html) for business-like digital services that are usually offered for payment, and [§ 18(1) MStV](https://www.die-medienanstalten.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Rechtsgrundlagen/Gesetze_Staatsvertraege/Medienstaatsvertrag_MStV.pdf) for any telemedia that is not purely personal or family. Either way Keyhole must show name and a serviceable postal address, an email address and a fast electronic contact under § 5 DDG, and a VAT ID if one exists. **Interpretation:** a public results service is not personal or family use, so § 18 MStV applies even if Keyhole is free. Whether a c/o or business address satisfies the address requirement instead of a home address is a question for a lawyer.

## Other operator duties

- **Record of processing activities.** The Article 30(5) exemption for organisations under 250 staff does not apply when processing is not occasional, and Keyhole processes continuously, so a record is required ([Article 30](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)).
- **Data protection officer.** Not required. § 38 BDSG requires one only when at least 20 persons regularly process personal data, and Article 37 targets large-scale monitoring or special category data.
- **Security and breaches.** Article 32 requires appropriate security, and Article 33 requires reporting a breach to the supervisory authority within 72 hours unless it is unlikely to result in a risk.
- **Supervisory authority.** The competent authority is the one for the German state where Keyhole is established.

## Erasure and objection against published results

A pilot can object to publication under Article 21(1). Keyhole must then stop unless it shows compelling legitimate grounds that override the pilot's interests, rights, and freedoms. A successful objection triggers erasure under Article 17(1)(c). Article 17(3) exempts processing needed for freedom of expression and information (a) and for legal claims (e), among others. The CJEU held in [Google Spain C-131/12](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-131/12) (para. 93) that initially lawful processing can become incompatible with the GDPR over time when the data are no longer necessary. Requests must be answered within one month under Article 12(3), and recipients must be notified under Article 19.

**Interpretation:** during a competition and its protest period, the integrity of results is a strong compelling ground and the legal-claims exemption may apply. Years later the balance shifts toward the pilot, especially for small events. Replacing the name with a neutral label while keeping the ranking, scores, and glider intact satisfies most requests without corrupting historical results. Removing a pilot's IGC file from public download is easier to justify than removing their ranking. Keyhole should design results so that one pilot's identity can be pseudonymised in place without recomputing or deleting the competition.

## Retention constraints

- **Storage limitation.** [Article 5(1)(e)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) allows identifiable storage only as long as necessary for the purposes. Longer storage is allowed only for archiving in the public interest, scientific or historical research, or statistics under Article 89(1) safeguards. **Interpretation:** a private results service is unlikely to qualify as archiving in the public interest without a lawyer's confirmation.
- **No statutory duty to keep IGC files or results.** No German or EU law requiring retention of competition results or flight logs was found. Sporting rules, such as protest and appeal windows or record claims, are not a legal obligation under Article 6(1)(c) or 17(3)(b), but they are a legitimate interest and can support the legal-claims exemption until those windows close.
- **Tax and commercial records.** If Keyhole charges fees, [§ 147(3) AO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ao_1977/__147.html) requires keeping books for 10 years, accounting vouchers for 8 years, and business letters and other tax-relevant documents for 6 years. That covers invoices and billing data, not results or IGC files.
- **Online publication duration.** German authority guidance expects online publication of external participants' results to end after an appropriate time that depends on the event's significance ([LfDI BW](https://www.baden-wuerttemberg.datenschutz.de/orientierungshilfe-datenschutz-verein/)).
- **Different periods per data type.** Raw IGC files, public results, admin accounts, login tokens, and server logs serve different purposes, so each needs its own justified period in the record and the privacy policy.

## Where a lawyer is needed

- Choosing between joint controllership, independent controllership, and processing, and drafting the organiser terms that follow from it.
- Confirming that a permanent public archive with downloadable IGC files passes the 6(1)(f) balancing test, or what limits make it pass.
- Reviewing the privacy policy, Impressum address, and organiser notice before launch.
- Reassessing US processors when the Court rules on C-703/25 P or the Commission acts on the EDPB's letter.
